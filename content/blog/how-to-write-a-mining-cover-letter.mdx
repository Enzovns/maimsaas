# How to Write a Mining Cover Letter That Gets You the Interview

Landing a mining job in Australia is competitive. You're up against hundreds of applicants for the same FIFO roster, the same underground role, or the same site supervisor position. Your cover letter is often the difference between your CV getting read or getting skipped.

Here's what actually works.

## 1. Lead with your ticket and site experience

Hiring managers in mining don't have time. They're scanning cover letters for qualifications first. Lead with your most relevant ticket or certification:

> "I hold a current QLD Open Cut Examiner ticket and have 7 years of surface mining experience across Bowen Basin thermal and coking coal operations."

That's the first sentence. Done.

## 2. Match the job description — word for word

If the JD says "CAT 793 haul truck operator", use that exact phrase. Many companies use ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) that keyword-match before a human ever sees your application.

## 3. Mention the roster upfront

FIFO vs DIDO vs residential isn't just a preference — it's an operational requirement. If you're applying for a 2/1 FIFO role, confirm you're comfortable with that arrangement:

> "I have worked 2 weeks on, 1 week off FIFO rotations for the past four years and am comfortable with this arrangement."

## 4. Keep it under 350 words

A cover letter is not your autobiography. One page max. Hiring managers at large mining companies read dozens per day. Get to the point.

## 5. Personalise every application

Generic cover letters get deleted. Reference the company name, the specific site if mentioned, and one thing about why you want to work for them.
